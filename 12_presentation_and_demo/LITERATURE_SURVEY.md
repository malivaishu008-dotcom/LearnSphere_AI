# Literature Survey - LearnSphere AI

## Overview

LearnSphere AI is informed by established ideas in educational technology, self-regulated learning, retrieval practice, learning analytics, and responsible AI design. The project does not claim to prove academic improvement in its current MVP stage. Instead, it translates research-supported learning behaviours into practical software modules that can be evaluated in a future pilot.

## Self-Regulated Learning

Self-regulated learning focuses on a student's ability to plan, monitor, and adapt learning behaviour. Students benefit when they can set goals, track effort, notice barriers, and revise their strategy. LearnSphere AI reflects this through the planner, dashboard, focus logging, daily diary, and recommendation surfaces.

## Retrieval Practice

Retrieval practice encourages students to recall information from memory rather than only rereading notes. This is a common evidence-backed learning strategy because it helps reveal gaps and strengthens long-term retention. LearnSphere AI supports this through short quiz generation and active-recall practice workflows.

## Spaced Revision and Study Planning

Students often delay revision until exams are near. Planning tools can help distribute work over time, but they must be simple enough for repeated daily use. LearnSphere AI uses subject-linked tasks, planned minutes, and timetable records to make the next study action visible.

## Learning Analytics

Learning analytics can help students understand patterns such as consistency, practice completion, and effort distribution. However, analytics can also mislead if they appear too certain. For this reason, LearnSphere AI frames consistency and mark scenarios as indicative signals. The application avoids presenting predicted marks as guaranteed outcomes.

## AI in Education

AI tools can support explanation, summarisation, question generation, and personalised feedback. At the same time, they introduce risks related to inaccurate output, overdependence, privacy, and unclear data sharing. LearnSphere AI therefore keeps the current MVP provider-neutral and documents a safer production route using server-managed credentials, consent, source-grounded responses, and evaluation.

## Design Implications

The literature direction leads to five implementation principles:

1. Keep planning close to the student's actual material.
2. Encourage active recall instead of passive consumption.
3. Make learning progress visible without increasing pressure.
4. Treat AI as support, not authority.
5. Make privacy and data boundaries clear before public deployment.
